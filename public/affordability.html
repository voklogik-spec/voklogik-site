Housing Ratio —
0%
Your monthly payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and HOA if any) divided by your gross monthly income. Typically comfortable at 28% or under; many loan programs allow more.
Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI) —
0%
Your monthly payment plus your other monthly debts, divided by your gross monthly income. Conventional guidelines often cap this around 36%, though many programs allow up to 43–50% with strong compensating factors.
Loan-to-Value (LTV) —
0%
Your loan amount divided by the property's value. On a purchase, lenders use the lower of the purchase price and the appraised value. On a conventional loan, mortgage insurance usually applies when this is above 80%.
Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV) —
0%
Your loan plus any other loans secured by the property (a second mortgage or home equity line), divided by the property's value. Lenders set their own CLTV limits by program.

If either ratio looks high, open Monthly Payment above and try a smaller loan amount or a lower rate.

About the income methods

What it covers

Two-year averages (with an optional, non-standard three-year average) of the income on your tax returns: sole proprietor, partnership, S corporation, C corporation, farm, rental and royalty income, plus bonus, commission, capital gains, and interest and dividends. Add-backs such as depreciation follow common cash-flow methods.

What it doesn't cover

Businesses under two years old, business liquidity tests, special treatment of money left inside a corporation, and each lender's own rules. Rental income is simplified. A loan officer or underwriter reviewing your actual returns will give you the real number.
